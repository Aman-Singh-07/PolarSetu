Facility Overview

Commissioned in March 2012 as India's third Antarctic research facility. Constructed from 134 prefabricated shipping containers within an aerodynamic envelope to withstand blizzards exceeding 200 km/h. Houses 47 scientists in summer and 25 over winter.

India's Cryospheric Footprint

Indian Polar & Himalayan Research Stations

Permanent observation stations managed by NCPOR under the Ministry of Earth Sciences across polar and high-altitude realms — ANTARCTICA: Bharati Station, Maitri Station • ARCTIC: Himadri Station • HIMALAYA: Himansh Station (Spiti Basin).

ANTARCTICA Active Year-Round

Bharati Station

69°24′S, 76°11′E

Located in Larsemann Hills, East Antarctica. Primary hub for oceanography, atmospheric chemistry, and satellite telemetry relays.

Est. 2012
ANTARCTICA Active Year-Round

Maitri Station

70°45′S, 11°44′E

Situated at Schirmacher Oasis. India's veteran research station monitoring limnological lake ecosystems, meteorological patterns, and deep geology.

Est. 1989
ARCTIC Seasonal / Winter

Himadri Station

78°55′N, 11°56′E

Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard, Norway. India’s premier Arctic observatory studying fjord dynamics, aerosol chemistry, and teleconnections to the Indian Monsoon.

Est. 2008
HIMALAYAS High-Altitude Base

Himansh Station (Spiti Basin)

32°24′N, 77°37′E

Located at Chandra Basin, Spiti Valley, Himachal Pradesh (4,080 m ASL). Focuses on Himalayan glacier melt, mass balance, and freshwater security.

Est. 2016
Ny-Ålesund Fjord Cryo-Drilling
Active Field Operation Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard Archipelago

Arctic Ice Coring & Green Laser Aerosol Lidar Profiling

During the 2024–25 winter campaign at Himadri Station, NCPOR scientists extract undisturbed sea-ice cores to quantify black carbon deposition and calibrate micro-pulse Raman LIDAR observations of polar stratospheric clouds.
